This is day one. The project did not exist this morning. It began as: *here is
https://particle-fluid-lab-3d.shyhoof.chatgpt.site/, can you make this a lava
lamp?* That site is a ChatGPT-built WebGPU/three.js Navier–Stokes demo shipped as
a minified bundle on a domain nobody here can redeploy to, so what happened
instead was: keep its lab-instrument shell — orbit viewport, HUD, a panel of SI
controls — and replace the physics entirely. Zero dependencies, one WebGL2 pass,
no build step.

**The one number.** Wax dosed ~1% denser than the aqueous phase at 20 °C,
expanding twice as fast with temperature, so the two weigh the same at

```
T_cross = 20 + (ρ_w20 − ρ_a20) / (ρ_w20 β_w − ρ_a20 β_a)  =  49.1 °C
```

Everything the lamp does is organised around that. Read
[PHYSICS.md](PHYSICS.md) §1–2 before touching anything; the window rule in §2 is
the point of the whole project.

## 2. The model, in one page

| file | what it owns |
|---|---|
| `js/params.js` | every constant, in SI, with each **calibrated** value labelled next to what it was calibrated against |
| `js/column.js` | the aqueous phase as a 1-D energy column, 96 slices; the convection closure; the return flow |
| `js/wax.js` | PBF + Akinci tension + per-particle T + latent heat + blob identity + the film |
| `js/lamp.js` | owns both clocks, the gated fast-forward, the window verdict, the resolution verdict |
| `js/volume.js` | splat particles to an RGBA8 3-D texture; alpha is a prefix-sum of light transmission |
| `js/render.js` | one raymarch pass: glass as a surface of revolution, fittings as SDF cones, wax as a volume |

Three couplings run between the phases, and each is the continuum result for a
drop of the blob's **actual** size rather than something invented per particle:
buoyancy per particle; drag per *blob* (quadratic, `Cd ≈ 1`); heat over the
blob's true surface area with `Nu = 2 + 0.6 Re^0.5 Pr^(1/3)`.

Two mechanisms exist only because the lamp did not work without them, and both
are load-bearing:

- **Return flow** (`Column.updateReturnFlow`). The globe is sealed, so
  `v_aq = −Q_wax/A_open`. Without it the wax rose through a fluid that never had
  to get out of the way, and a coalesced slug behaved like a piston in a
  frictionless bore.
- **The film** (`Wax.relabel`, `SOLVER.disjoinK`, `SOLVER.drainTime`). Surfactant
  films resist coalescence. Without it the wax merged into one mass within thirty
  seconds, and a mass that big has `τ = ρca/3h ≈ 30 minutes` — the lamp stopped
  being a lava lamp.

**Verified this morning**, all with the shipping parameters:

| | |
|---|---|
| steady state, 25 W, 22 °C room | plate **55.2** · base 52.0 · bulk 45.4 · cap **40.4 °C**, gradient 11.6 K |
| window | OPEN: `T_melt 34 < T_top 40.4 < T_cross 49.1 < T_plate 55.2` |
| cold plug, 10 steps | max speed decaying 0.51 → 0.27 cm/s, **0 clamps**, com steady at 1.36 cm, ρ/ρ₀ ≤ 1.057 |
| cold start | fast-forward skips **118.6 min** and stops at liftoff, wax 82% molten |
| energy, end of a 9-min run | 16.3 W in / 14.3 W out |
| browser, 1800 particles @ 41 Hz | step **7.09 ms**, draw **2.42 ms** → **39%** of a second at 718×772 |

## 3. What was wrong, and the symptom that exposed each

Twelve defects. They are listed by what they looked like, because that is how the
next one will be found.

### 3.1 The artificial pressure term detonated the fluid on the first step

Macklin & Müller quote `s_corr` as a bare constant because *their* λ is O(0.1).
Here λ is O(1e-6), so the term ran **a thousand times** the correction it is
meant to nudge. Symptom: particles teleporting the full height of the globe in
one step, reported as a 16.4 m/s "rise speed". It is now scaled to `lamScale`.

### 3.2 The density constraint was sucking the free surface inward

A liquid resists compression, not expansion. Free-surface particles sit at
ρ < ρ₀, and a lone particle has `Σ|∇C|² → 0` with `C ≈ −0.8`, so the correction
goes to infinity. `λ` is now zero wherever `C ≤ 0`.

### 3.3 The curvature term ran ~600× the cohesion it trims

Akinci's single γ is not dimensionally closed — the cohesion and curvature
expressions do not carry the same units, so one constant cannot scale both.
Written literally it blew a free drop apart in 0.1 s. Curvature is now anchored
to the cohesion spline's own magnitude `C(0)`, leaving `curvK` a shape parameter
of order one.

### 3.4 Stokes drag used the particle radius where the physics uses the blob

`4.5μ/(ρ rp²)` for rp = 1.3 mm is **27 /s** against Lamb's **0.22 /s** for a
15 mm drop — fourteen times over, enough to kill a shape oscillation inside a
fifth of its period. Same formula; wrong length. It now uses the blob radius,
which lands at 0.20 /s.

### 3.5 The window diagnostic called a running lamp stalled

It tested the plate against `T_cross` instead of against `T_rise(T_base)`. The
crossover is where the phases match at the *same* temperature; at the base the
fluid is hot and already light, so the wax has a lower bar. See PHYSICS.md §2.

### 3.6 Three render bugs, none of which showed up in a number

- **Upside down.** `up = right × fwd` instead of `fwd × right` gives a
  left-handed basis. It looks *almost plausible* — a lava lamp inverted is still
  a bright glow at one end of a glass column.
- **The base plate drew in front of the wax pool.** A bone-white disc through
  four centimetres of wax. Then, once the depth order was fixed, front-facing
  metal composited the *background* colour and the fittings vanished entirely,
  because the shading was computed only in the `!metalFront` branch.
- **Fresnel taken up to a centimetre inside the glass.** The "surface normal" at
  the first sample inside the bounding cylinder points somewhere between the
  glass and the axis, which put a sheen across the whole bottle and made the
  fluid look like milk. Now bisected to the real surface in six steps.

### 3.7 Substepping created energy out of a loop counter

`depositWaxHeat` accumulates **power**, and the column applies it over the whole
outer `dt`. Substepping without a `dt_sub/dt` weight hands the fluid `sub` times
the heat the wax gave up. Found only because the plate later read **−861 °C**.

### 3.8 The column's substep count was unbounded

It was chosen once from the state on entry. A sharp transient made that limit
tiny, the call demanded ~10⁴ substeps, and the process simply hung — twice, for
two minutes each, looking like an infinite loop. It is now adaptive per pass,
with the guard **counted** in `stiffSteps`.

### 3.9 Wax conduction has its own limit, and it tightens as dx shrinks

Rescaling the globe took the particle spacing from 4.5 to 3.5 mm and the
conduction limit from 20 s to 12 s, at which point the warm start's 20-second
relaxation step went unstable. `heatDtMax` is now derived from the diagonal of
the discrete operator summed over a rest lattice, so it follows the parameters.

### 3.10 The capillary CFL — the one worth remembering

Explicit surface tension carries `dt ≤ 0.25 √(ρh³/2πσ)`. A hardcoded 1/30 s was
**1.77× over** it. This does not look like divergence:

| | 1/30 s (1.77× over) | 1/41 s (from the condition) |
|---|---|---|
| velocity-clamp events in 12 s of lamp time | **660,996** | **8** |
| what it looked like | a lamp that seemed merely sluggish | — |

The step is now computed every frame from the smoothing length and whatever the
tension slider says, so moving σ from 4.5 to 8 mN/m visibly takes the solver from
41 Hz to 54 Hz.

### 3.11 Two metrics that were measuring their own rig

- **Cycle period** counted up-swings of the centre of mass with a minimum gap
  between them, and faithfully reported **13 s** — the gap — for a lamp whose
  blobs were plainly taking minutes. Now a detrended spectrum with a prominence
  gate that returns `null` rather than name a period.
- **Rise speed** was a running maximum of the fastest particle over the whole
  run, which reports the worst transient a run ever had and calls it a speed. Now
  the volume-weighted median **while in transit**, because a blob sits still for
  most of a cycle and a run-median measures the sitting.

### 3.12 The resolution verdict said RESOLVED about a quantity it had not measured

With nothing buoyant in the tank there is no Δρ to build a capillary length from.
The floored value came out as a confident 30 mm blob and a *resolved* verdict. It
now returns `known: false`.

## 4. The configuration is a choice, and it is the interesting part

`dt ~ n^(-1/2)` from §3.10, and `h ~ (V/n)^(1/3)`, so

```
compute per second of lamp time  ~  n^1.5
```

You pay for the particles **and** for the shorter step they force. Doubling the
count nearly triples the bill. That, not memory, is the ceiling on a real-time
particle lava lamp, and it is why the default is 1800 rather than as many as
fits.

Everything else follows from wanting the capillary length inside what 1800
particles can resolve (three particle spacings across a radius):

| choice | value | why not the obvious thing |
|---|---|---|
| globe | 14.5-inch, 486 mL | the 16.3-inch was built first: 3 h to develop, a wax charge that coalesces to nearly the bore width, and a fixed particle budget spread thinner |
| bulb | 25 W | 40 W in a globe this small drives the cap past 49.1 °C and the lamp stalls. 25 W is what that globe actually ships with — the energy budget asked for it independently |
| wax | 60 mL (12%) | real lamps run 15–25%. A coalesced 110 mL mass is a 30 mm sphere in a 33 mm bore and can only be a piston |
| σ | 4.5 mN/m | top of the physical 1–5 band, not the middle |

**All four are stated in PHYSICS.md §5 and the readout flips to UNDER-RESOLVED
the moment you move them.** That is the deal: the numbers were not tuned to make
a verdict pass, the verdict follows the numbers and says so out loud. Do not
quietly move any of them without re-running §1.

## 5. Traps

| What it looks like | What it actually is |
|---|---|
| A constant copied from a paper that immediately explodes | The paper's other quantities are in different units than yours. Macklin's `s_corr` assumes λ ~ 0.1; scale it to *your* λ, not theirs. |
| A solver that seems sluggish, not unstable | Check the clamp counter before the physics. 660,996 clamp events read as "a bit slow"; the real problem was a CFL condition nobody had written down. |
| A drag coefficient 14× too large | Ask which length the formula wants. Stokes drag on a blob is not Stokes drag on a particle, and the formula looks identical either way. |
| A diagnostic that says stalled while the thing plainly runs | It is comparing against a global threshold where the physics uses a local one. |
| A render that is wrong but plausible | Check the handedness. `up = fwd × right`, and the other order still produces a lit, coherent, upside-down picture. |
| Something composites as the background colour | A branch that computes the shading only in the case where it is *not* needed. |
| A number that agrees with your theory | Ask what would make it disagree. "Rise speed" as a running max agrees with any theory of a fast lamp because it reports the worst single instant. |
| A period that comes out suspiciously near a parameter | It is measuring the parameter. A turning-point counter with a minimum gap reports the gap. |
| A verdict that is confident about something not present | The floored default. With no buoyant wax there is no Δρ; report "not measurable", never the floor. |
| An instrument that was right last week | Re-run it after every model change. Adding the anti-coalescence film shattered the calibration puddle into 71 pieces and changed what the tool measured, silently. |
| Energy appearing from nowhere after a refactor | A per-substep loop depositing a **power** that the outer step then integrates over the full `dt`. |
| A process that hangs with no error | An unbounded substep count computed from a transient state. Bound it and count the binding. |
| A green run that another green run contradicts | Nothing is seeded. See §6.1. |
| A coefficient that was calibrated, on a rig that is not the shipping rig | Calibrate at the configuration you ship. `cohK` was fitted at a 2.15 mm spacing and the lamp runs at 3.22 mm; the effective quantity was 2.4× off and nothing complained. |
| A system that behaves erratically rather than wrongly | Check where it sits relative to its own stated thresholds. This lamp equilibrates 0.3 K below its rise threshold, so the dwell is set by a residual heating rate and the period is whatever noise decides. |

## 6. Known-weak, deliberately recorded rather than fixed

### 6.1 Two claims shipped this morning were stronger than the evidence

Both were found by re-running instruments after the model had moved, and both are
now corrected in the README, PHYSICS.md and `params.js`. They are the reason this
handoff opens with "green, with two numbers that need correcting".

**The 128-second cycle was n = 1, and it is an outlier.** Three warm-start runs
of the identical configuration:

| run | lamp time | cycle | in transit | rise while moving |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 9 min | **128 s** | — | — |
| B | 9 min | *not resolved* | 4 / 36 samples | 0.40 cm/s |
| C | 22 min | *not resolved* | 8 / 88 samples | 0.38 cm/s |

The spectral gate refused twice and was right to; the README quoted the one run
that answered. **The lamp is in transit for about a tenth of its life.**

The cause turned out to be worth more than the correction. The shipping
configuration **sits on the lower edge of its own window rule**: the pool
equilibrates near 47.6 °C against a 47.9 °C rise threshold at the base, so whether
and when it lifts is decided by a residual heating rate of hundredths of a kelvin
per minute. A lamp running slightly too cool is exactly this state, and the
window readout names it correctly — it is simply a poor default. BACKLOG §2.
Compounding it, nothing is seeded (BACKLOG §3), so no two runs are the same run.

**The cohesion coefficient is wrong at the spacing the lamp ships with.**
`cohK = 16.1` was fitted when σ was 3.0 mN/m on a puddle at a 2.15 mm particle
spacing. σ is now 4.5 and the lamp runs at 3.22 mm:

| spacing | σ target | σ_eff |
|---|---|---|
| 2.15 mm | 3.0 | 3.0 |
| 2.15 mm | 4.5 | 3.0–3.8 |
| **3.22 mm (as shipped)** | **4.5** | **1.9** |

The wax is held together by ~2.4× less tension than the panel says. The direction
is exactly what the scaling flaw predicts — per-pair cohesion goes as `dx^-2`
where the Laplace argument asks for `dx^-1` — so pairwise SPH surface tension is
resolution dependent and this coefficient does not transfer between spacings.

Getting there took two separate discoveries, and the second only appeared because
of the first. Re-running the calibration at all showed the **anti-coalescence film
shattering the calibration puddle into 71 pieces**: every fragment took a fresh
blob id and was then held apart by its own film, so the tool was measuring
thickness across a broken sheet. Adding a mechanism had silently changed what an
instrument measured. With the film switched off (it calibrates cohesion, not the
film) the puddle behaved — and *then* re-running at the lamp's own spacing
produced the table above, with the tool refusing to fit at all because a 28 mL
puddle at that spacing is under five particles deep.

It is **not** retuned in place: raising `cohK` 2.4× tightens the capillary CFL by
√2.4 (41 → ~64 Hz), takes the frame budget from 39% to ~60%, and changes blob
detachment — invalidating every measured number in §2. BACKLOG §1 has the shape of
the real fix, which is a corrected scaling rather than a new constant.

### 6.2 The rest

- **One blob, not five or six.** A real lava lamp mode, not the busiest one, and
  BACKLOG §1 has the experiment that would tell you whether the barrier is
  coalescence or pinch-off resolution.
- **The return flow moves volume but not heat.** It matters most exactly when the
  wax is moving fastest.
- **`nuC` is a fit, not a constant.** It had to be refitted from 1.2 to 1.8 when
  the globe was rescaled, which is the plainest evidence of what it is.
- **The velocity clamp is a safety net, not physics.** It counts, and the count is
  in `diagnostics()` and on the panel, so a run that leaned on it says so.
- **No mutation testing has been done at all.** Every "the instrument refuses"
  claim in this document is a claim about code I wrote and never tried to break.
  BACKLOG §3.

*The thing that kept happening today was measurement error dressed as physics.
The fluid detonating looked like a bad timestep and was a constant borrowed from
a paper whose λ is five orders of magnitude from mine. The lamp looking sluggish
was a CFL condition. The 13-second cycle was the minimum gap in the detector. The
"resolved" verdict was a floored default. In every case the number was doing what
it was built to do and the thing it was built to do was not the thing I wanted to
know.*

*The countermeasure that actually worked was making instruments refuse. The
calibration tool that will not fit an exponent through under-resolved puddles
found the film interaction that a tool willing to average anything would have
smoothed over; the cycle detector that returns null told me my README was
overclaiming. Refusals are more useful than answers, because an answer cannot
tell you it should not have been asked.*
